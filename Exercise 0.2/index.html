Why appliance energy consumption matters

As households adopt larger screens and more sophisticated appliances, the hidden energy cost becomes harder to see. It is not only about switching devices off — it is about the efficiency built into the technology we bring into our homes in the first place.

This project looks at how measurable attributes, such as the screen size of a television, relate to annual energy draw and long-term running costs.

Frequently asked questions

Appliance energy consumption is the amount of electrical energy a household device uses over a period of time. It is normally measured in kilowatt hours (kWh) and is a primary factor in a residential electricity bill.

The area of a screen is related to how much light it has to produce. Larger screens generally need more power to illuminate, which tends to raise energy consumption even when the panel technology is efficient.

An efficient appliance uses less electricity to do the same job, which can reduce running costs over the life of the product and lower the energy a household draws overall.

Energy use in kilowatt hours is multiplied by the electricity price. Retail tariffs are usually quoted in cents per kilowatt hour, so the price is divided by 100 to work in dollars.
